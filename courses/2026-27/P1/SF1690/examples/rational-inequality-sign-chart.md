Solve $\frac4{x+3}-2\ge0$. Put the expression over one denominator: $$\frac{4-2(x+3)}{x+3}=\frac{-2x-2}{x+3}\ge0.$$ The critical values are $x=-3$ (undefined, exclude it) and $x=-1$ (zero, include it). Testing the intervals gives negative on $(-\infty,-3)$, positive on $(-3,-1)$, and negative on $(-1,\infty)$. Therefore the solution is $$(-3,-1].$$
