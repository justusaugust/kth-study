Let $p(x)=x^3-3x^2+4$. Testing $x=-1$ gives $-1-3+4=0$, so $x+1$ is a factor.

Division gives $p(x)=(x+1)(x^2-4x+4)=(x+1)(x-2)^2$.

The roots are $-1$ (multiplicity one) and $2$ (multiplicity two). The graph crosses at $-1$ and touches at $2$. Its leading term is $x^3$, so it falls to the left and rises to the right. Check by expanding: $(x+1)(x^2-4x+4)=x^3-3x^2+4$.
