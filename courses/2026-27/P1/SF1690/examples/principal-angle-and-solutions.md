Solve $\sin\theta=-\sqrt3/2$ for $0\le\theta<2\pi$.

The principal angle is $\alpha=\arcsin(-\sqrt3/2)=-\pi/3$. It is outside the requested interval, but provides the reference value.

The two families are $\theta=-\pi/3+2k\pi$ and $\theta=4\pi/3+2k\pi$. In the requested interval, they give $5\pi/3$ and $4\pi/3$ respectively. Both lie below the horizontal axis on the unit circle, so their sine is negative.

Answer: $\theta\in\{4\pi/3,5\pi/3\}$. Returning only the calculator's $-\pi/3$ would miss both the interval constraint and the second solution.
