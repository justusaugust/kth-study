Let $f(x)=(x-1)^2+2$ with $x\ge1$. Its range is $[2,\infty)$.

From $y-2=(x-1)^2$, the restriction implies $x-1\ge0$, so $x=1+\sqrt{y-2}$:

$$f^{-1}(x)=1+\sqrt{x-2},\qquad x\ge2.$$

The inverse has range $[1,\infty)$. On the alternative original domain $x\le1$, we would instead obtain $1-\sqrt{x-2}$, with the same inverse domain $[2,\infty)$ but a different range.
