Solve $w^3=-8$ over the complex numbers.

Write $-8=8(\cos\pi+i\sin\pi)$. Every root has modulus $8^{1/3}=2$, and the angles are

$$
\alpha_k=\frac{\pi+2k\pi}{3},\qquad k=0,1,2.
$$

These give $\pi/3,\pi,5\pi/3$. In rectangular form the roots are

$$
1+i\sqrt3,\qquad -2,\qquad 1-i\sqrt3.
$$

They are equally spaced by $2\pi/3$ on the radius-$2$ circle. Cubing multiplies each angle by three, giving $\pi,3\pi,5\pi$, all pointing to the negative real axis; each cube has modulus $8$.

The real cube root is $-2$, but it is only one of the three complex roots. Listing $k=3$ would repeat $k=0$.
