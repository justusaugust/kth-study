## How to use this review

The course plan assigns Lecture 16 to review of the course. This page is an authored revision session based on the lessons already here, not a record of what was said in class.

Work through the six sections below without notes first. Open a self-check, attempt it on paper, then use its hints or solution. If you cannot choose a method or explain a step, revisit the topic and worked example before trying again. Use **Revisit this question** to collect those tasks in the [practice queue](/practice?course=sf1690).

For exam rules, dates, past papers and a timed revision plan, use the [SF1690 exam guide](/courses/sf1690/exam). This review focuses on the mathematics.

## 1. Numbers, inequalities and coordinate geometry · Lectures 1–2

Keep track of which operations preserve equivalence. Multiplying an inequality by a negative number reverses its direction; dividing by an expression requires knowing whether it is zero and what sign it has. Absolute value is distance, so an outside-distance condition usually produces two intervals.

Revisit [real numbers and intervals](/courses/sf1690/concepts/real-numbers-and-lines), [absolute value](/courses/sf1690/concepts/absolute-value), [lines and slope](/courses/sf1690/concepts/lines-and-slopes), and [coordinates, distance and circles](/courses/sf1690/concepts/cartesian-distance-circles).

- **Try:** [Reverse an inequality correctly](/courses/sf1690/concepts/real-numbers-and-lines#question-inequality-negative-factor).
- **Try:** [Identify a circle by completing the square](/courses/sf1690/concepts/cartesian-distance-circles#question-circle-completing-square).
- **Go deeper:** [Parabolas and shifts](/courses/sf1690/concepts/parabolas-and-shifts), [quadratic functions](/courses/sf1690/concepts/quadratic-functions), and [ellipses and hyperbolas](/courses/sf1690/concepts/ellipses-and-hyperbolas).

Before moving on, explain how an equation distinguishes the boundary of a circle from its interior, and why exchanging the two points does not change a line's slope.

## 2. Functions, composition and inverses · Lectures 3–4

A formula alone does not specify a function: its domain matters. In $f\circ g$, the input must be accepted by $g$, and the resulting value must be accepted by $f$. To construct an inverse, first choose a one-to-one domain; then exchange input and output and check the resulting branch.

Revisit [domain and range](/courses/sf1690/concepts/functions-domain-and-range), [the vertical-line test](/courses/sf1690/concepts/function-graphs-and-vertical-line-test), [even and odd symmetry](/courses/sf1690/concepts/even-odd-function-symmetry), [function arithmetic](/courses/sf1690/concepts/function-arithmetic-and-domains), [composition](/courses/sf1690/concepts/function-composition), [piecewise functions](/courses/sf1690/concepts/piecewise-defined-functions), and [inverses](/courses/sf1690/concepts/inverse-functions).

- **Try:** [Calculate both composition orders and domains](/courses/sf1690/concepts/function-composition#question-composition-order-and-domain).
- **Compare your method:** [Choose the inverse square-root branch](/courses/sf1690/concepts/inverse-functions#example-invert-shifted-square).

Explain why $f^{-1}$ is not $1/f$, and why checking only the outer formula's domain is insufficient for a composition.

## 3. Polynomial factors and rational restrictions · Lectures 5–6

Factoring connects an algebraic expression to its roots and graph. Repeated factors explain whether a graph crosses or touches the axis. Cancelling a factor simplifies values where the original expression was defined; it does not restore an excluded input.

Revisit [polynomial roots and factors](/courses/sf1690/concepts/polynomial-roots-and-factors) and [rational functions and cancellation](/courses/sf1690/concepts/rational-functions-and-cancellation).

- **Try:** [Factor and count roots](/courses/sf1690/concepts/polynomial-roots-and-factors#question-factor-and-count-roots).
- **Try:** [Cancel without changing the domain](/courses/sf1690/concepts/rational-functions-and-cancellation#question-cancel-without-changing-domain).
- **Compare your method:** [Separate a hole from a pole](/courses/sf1690/concepts/rational-functions-and-cancellation#example-rational-hole-and-pole).

Check a factorisation by expanding it. Check a proposed solution in the original equation, especially after squaring or cancelling.

## 4. Trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms · Lectures 7–9

Start a trigonometric equation with all solution families, then restrict to the required interval. An inverse trigonometric function returns a principal value, not every possible angle. Logarithms require positive arguments; exponential substitutions also impose restrictions.

Revisit [radians and the unit circle](/courses/sf1690/concepts/radians-and-unit-circle), [trigonometric graphs and equations](/courses/sf1690/concepts/trigonometric-graphs-and-equations), [inverse trigonometric functions](/courses/sf1690/concepts/inverse-trigonometric-functions), and [exponential, logarithmic and hyperbolic functions](/courses/sf1690/concepts/exponential-logarithmic-hyperbolic-functions).

- **Try:** [Keep every solution when factoring a trig equation](/courses/sf1690/concepts/trigonometric-graphs-and-equations#question-trig-all-solutions).
- **Compare your method:** [Reject a root outside the logarithm domain](/courses/sf1690/concepts/exponential-logarithmic-hyperbolic-functions#example-logarithm-equation-domain).
- **Try:** [Check a hyperbolic identity](/courses/sf1690/concepts/exponential-logarithmic-hyperbolic-functions#question-hyperbolic-identity-check).

Check angles on the unit circle, not only on a memorised formula. Be able to explain why $\log(a+b)$ cannot generally be replaced by $\log a+\log b$.

## 5. Complex numbers and roots · Lectures 10–11

Use Cartesian form for addition and subtraction, the conjugate for division, and polar form for powers and roots. The quadrant matters when finding an argument. A nonzero complex number has exactly $n$ distinct $n$th roots, equally spaced in angle; zero has no argument.

Revisit [complex arithmetic and polar form](/courses/sf1690/concepts/complex-number-basics) and [De Moivre and complex roots](/courses/sf1690/concepts/de-moivre-and-complex-roots).

- **Compare your method:** [Find and plot all cube roots](/courses/sf1690/concepts/de-moivre-and-complex-roots#example-complex-cube-roots).
- **Try:** [Count distinct roots and handle zero](/courses/sf1690/concepts/de-moivre-and-complex-roots#question-complex-fourth-roots).

Count your roots, check their angular spacing, and raise one back to the required power. If the modulus or argument is wrong, changing the root index will not repair it.

## 6. Vectors, lines, planes and area · Lectures 12–15

Choose the product by the job: a dot product gives a scalar and tests orthogonality; a cross product gives an oriented normal in three dimensions. A line needs a point and direction; a plane needs a point and nonzero normal. Two independent edge vectors can give that normal.

Revisit [components and lengths](/courses/sf1690/concepts/vectors-and-components), [dot products and projection](/courses/sf1690/concepts/dot-products-and-orthogonality), [lines and planes](/courses/sf1690/concepts/vector-lines-and-planes), and [cross products and area](/courses/sf1690/concepts/cross-products-and-area).

- **Try:** [Check a point with one shared line parameter](/courses/sf1690/concepts/vector-lines-and-planes#question-line-membership-shared-parameter).
- **Compare your method:** [Compute and verify an oriented normal](/courses/sf1690/concepts/cross-products-and-area#example-cross-product-normal-check).
- **Try:** [Reverse a cross product without changing the area](/courses/sf1690/concepts/cross-products-and-area#question-cross-order-area).

Check a normal against both edge vectors using dot products. Substituting a line into a plane can give one intersection, no intersection, or the entire line. Check zero-vector and parallel cases before dividing or normalising.

## Finish with a mixed pass

Choose one task from each section and work without notes. For each solution, write the method, the conditions, and an independent check. Do not mark a topic ready just because the solution looks familiar.
