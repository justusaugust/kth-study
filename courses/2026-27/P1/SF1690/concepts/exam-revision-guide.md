## Start with a diagnostic, not a reread

Spend 35 minutes without notes on five tasks: a rational equation with a domain restriction; a trigonometric equation with all solutions; a complex root; a line–plane intersection; and an area computed with a cross product. Choose unfamiliar values. Explain each step on paper, then compare with the relevant worked example. A topic belongs in your revision queue if you cannot start it, lose a restriction, or cannot justify the method.

The practice bank contains course self-checks, not official past-exam questions or a prediction of the paper. [Open SF1690 practice](/practice?course=sf1690). Use the official course room for past papers and solutions.

## Algebra and functions · Lectures 1–6

Review [real numbers and inequalities](/courses/sf1690/concepts/real-numbers-and-lines), [coordinate geometry](/courses/sf1690/concepts/cartesian-distance-circles), [functions and domains](/courses/sf1690/concepts/functions-domain-and-range), [composition](/courses/sf1690/concepts/function-composition), [polynomial factors](/courses/sf1690/concepts/polynomial-roots-and-factors) and [rational cancellation](/courses/sf1690/concepts/rational-functions-and-cancellation).

You should be able to solve inequalities with a sign chart, complete a square, read a conic from its equation, compose functions in the stated order, restrict an inverse to a one-to-one branch, and distinguish a removable hole from a pole.

A cancelled denominator still excludes its original zeros. Squaring an equation can introduce extraneous roots. Test every candidate in the original expression. For an inverse, state both its domain and range; an inverse function is not a reciprocal.

**Paper check:** factor a cubic with one repeated root, sketch its crossings and touches, then cancel a common factor in a rational expression and state the unchanged domain.

## Trigonometry and elementary functions · Lectures 7–9

Work through [radians and the unit circle](/courses/sf1690/concepts/radians-and-unit-circle), [trigonometric equations](/courses/sf1690/concepts/trigonometric-graphs-and-equations), [inverse trigonometric functions](/courses/sf1690/concepts/inverse-trigonometric-functions) and [exponential, logarithmic and hyperbolic functions](/courses/sf1690/concepts/exponential-logarithmic-hyperbolic-functions).

Know exact unit-circle values, parity, periods and principal inverse ranges. Derive a solution family before restricting to an interval. An inverse sine gives one principal angle, not every solution of a sine equation. Logarithm arguments must be positive, and a logarithm of a sum cannot be split into a sum of logarithms.

**Paper check:** solve a double-angle equation on a full period, invert a shifted exponential, and verify a hyperbolic identity from the exponential definitions.

## Complex numbers · Lectures 10–11

Review [complex arithmetic](/courses/sf1690/concepts/complex-number-basics) and [De Moivre and roots](/courses/sf1690/concepts/de-moivre-and-complex-roots).

Switch confidently between Cartesian and polar form. Use the conjugate for division, choose the argument by quadrant, and enumerate every distinct root. For a nonzero number with modulus $r$ and argument $\theta$, its $n$ roots have modulus $r^{1/n}$ and arguments $(\theta+2k\pi)/n$, for $k=0,\ldots,n-1$. Zero has no argument.

**Paper check:** divide two non-real numbers, take an integer power in polar form, then find and plot all fourth roots of a nonzero complex number. Count the roots and check one by substitution.

## Vector geometry · Lectures 12–15

Use [vectors and components](/courses/sf1690/concepts/vectors-and-components), [dot products](/courses/sf1690/concepts/dot-products-and-orthogonality), [lines and planes](/courses/sf1690/concepts/vector-lines-and-planes) and [cross products](/courses/sf1690/concepts/cross-products-and-area).

A dot product is a scalar; a cross product is a vector in three dimensions. A direction vector belongs to a line, while a normal vector belongs to a plane. Check whether an angle is defined before dividing by lengths: the zero vector has no direction. For line–plane intersections, substitute the line into the plane and distinguish one intersection, none, and a line contained in the plane.

**Paper check:** find a plane through three non-collinear points, intersect a line with it, compute the triangle's area, and verify the normal is orthogonal to two independent edges. Reverse the order of a cross product to check its sign.

## A revision sequence for the remaining week

1. **First pass:** do the diagnostic and repair algebra/domain mistakes. Rework failed tasks with different numbers.
2. **Next two sessions:** cover trigonometry and complex numbers, then vectors and geometry. Use a visual when it helps you understand a method; finish with a closed-book calculation.
3. **Timed rehearsal:** solve an official past paper in three hours, without books or a calculator if the exam rules remain unchanged. Do not inspect its solutions halfway through.
4. **Correction session:** classify each lost point as a method gap, algebra slip, missing condition or unclear presentation. Re-solve the whole task, not just the wrong line.
5. **Final day:** practise the weakest two methods, review exact values and conditions, and confirm location, registration and permitted aids.

Do not chase new material at the expense of methods you still cannot carry out independently. A correct answer with an unexplained leap is less dependable than a short, justified chain of reasoning.
